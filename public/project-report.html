PHASE - I: PROJECT INITIATION & LIFE CYCLE MODEL

Title: Campus Property Recovery Protocol (KIIT-Find)

Problem Definition:

Across the 24 sprawling campuses of KIIT Deemed to be University—encompassing lecture halls, computer labs, central libraries, food courts (Kathajodi, Chintan), sports facilities (KSAC), and student hostels (KP-1 to KP-20, QC-1 to QC-12)—hundreds of high-value items (smartphones, Apple AirPods, scientific calculators, KIIT Smart ID cards, and laptops) are misplaced every semester by more than 40,000 enrolled students.

Currently, lost-and-found recovery relies completely on unmonitored WhatsApp groups and informal Telegram channels. This leads to four critical software and institutional failures:

  1. Zero Ownership Verification: Anyone reading a public post can claim an item by repeating the description back to the finder.
  2. Severe PII Privacy Leaks: Photographs of lost KIIT Smart ID cards are posted unredacted, exposing student roll numbers, blood groups, photographs, and phone numbers in violation of India's DPDP Act 2023.
  3. Listing Spam & Clutter: Unverified duplicate posts, prank listings, and spam flood chat channels, burying genuine recovery requests.
  4. Unsafe Custody Handover: Students meet strangers in unverified hostel rooms with zero chain-of-custody documentation or security oversight.

Therefore, the university administration requires an automated software platform (KIIT-Find) that authenticates institutional students via @kiit.ac.in credentials, enforces zero-knowledge ownership verification, automatically protects sensitive PII, governs listing volume with anti-spam rate limiting, and authenticates physical handovers at designated Campus Safe Zones using single-use cryptographic QR tokens.

Abstract

The Campus Property Recovery Protocol (KIIT-Find) is an enterprise web software system engineered to automate, secure, and streamline the recovery of lost personal and academic property across all 24 campuses of KIIT Deemed to be University.

When a student discovers an unattended item on campus, they log in via their authenticated university credentials (@kiit.ac.in) and record the item's spatial coordinates (Campus number, building, and landmark). For sensitive items such as student ID cards, the software automatically blurs identity fields to prevent privacy exposure. Crucially, the finder configures a Concealed Challenge Question (or records a hardware identifier) without revealing the answer publicly.

To claim an item, a claimant must satisfy the Zero-Trust Multi-Vector Ownership Protocol (ZK-MOP). Claimants submit non-public proof: answering the concealed challenge, matching a hardware serial/IMEI evaluated via SHA-256 cryptographic hashing, providing a pre-loss purchase invoice, or pledging a physical biometric unlock challenge at the checkpoint. Once approved, the system generates an HMAC-SHA256 signed, time-expiring (24-hour) Handover QR Pass.

The physical exchange is completed at an official Campus Safe Zone (e.g., Campus 15 Security Gate or Central Library Helpdesk), where the finder or security officer scans the QR pass using an integrated in-browser camera scanner. The transaction is instantly finalized, and an immutable log is written to the system audit ledger. Overall, KIIT-Find replaces vulnerable informal communication with an institutional, privacy-preserving, and mathematically verifiable campus recovery infrastructure.

Model Chosen: Incremental Process Model

In the Incremental Model, the software is developed and delivered in multiple sequential increments. Each increment progresses systematically through the core phases of the software engineering lifecycle: requirements analysis, system design, implementation, testing, deployment, and customer evaluation. Upon completing and releasing one increment, the subsequent increment builds upon the working codebase until the full system is obtained.

Why we chose this model:

  • Natural Modular Decomposition: KIIT-Find decomposes cleanly into distinct functional subsystems (Reporting & Spatial Coordinates, ZK-MOP Verification Engine, Dynamic QR Handover Pass, Anti-Spam Quota Engine). Each subsystem can be designed, implemented, and tested independently before integration.
  • Early Availability of Core Functional Features: In campus operations, the most critical capability is allowing students to report found items and search the active catalog. With the incremental approach, Increment 1 delivered a functioning portal to students immediately, while the more sophisticated cryptographic QR scanning modules followed in subsequent increments.
  • Facilitates Academic Feedback & Requirement Refinement: During Phase-I review, evaluators raised edge cases surrounding false claims and listing spam. The incremental model allowed us to incorporate the anti-spam quota system, velocity cooldowns, and the 4-tier ZK-MOP verification matrix in Increment 2 without having to discard the core database schemas established in Increment 1.
  • Risk Mitigation for Camera Hardware: Integrating in-browser WebRTC optical barcode scanning (html5-qrcode) involves device compatibility risks across diverse student smartphones. Developing the scanning module as an isolated third increment ensured that camera permission quirks would not block progress on the primary data pipeline.

PHASE - II: SOFTWARE REQUIREMENT SPECIFICATION (SRS)

2.1 Introduction

Many campus departments and hostel desks still manage lost property through handwritten paper registers or chaotic social media group chats, which are slow, prone to errors, and lack any verification mechanism. The Campus Property Recovery Protocol (KIIT-Find) is engineered to automate the entire lifecycle of lost and found property across KIIT's 24 campuses.

This document specifies the functional requirements, non-functional constraints, and future implementation goals of KIIT-Find, adhering strictly to the black-box specification paradigm—describing only the external input/output behavior and processing logic without coupling to internal implementation artifacts.

2.2 Functional Requirements (R1 to R5)

The functional requirements are structured into a set of high-level requirements R1 to R5, each broken down into fine-grained sub-requirements specifying exact Input, Output, and Processing:

R1: Report Lost or Found Campus Property with Privacy Masking

Description: Enables an authenticated student or staff member to register a lost or found item with spatial coordinates, apply privacy masking to identity cards, and configure a concealed challenge question.

R1.1: Capture Item Details & Spatial Coordinates
Input: Item title, category, description, campus location (Campus 1–24), specific landmark.
Output: Validated item preview displayed to user.
Processing: Validates title length ($\ge 3$ characters), binds listing to reporter's authenticated @kiit.ac.in email.

R1.2: Privacy Masking of Sensitive Personal Data (PII)
Input: Uploaded photograph of item and category selection.
Output: Public catalog image with identity credentials masked.
Processing: If category is KIIT_ID_CARD or WALLET_DOCS, flags isSensitive = true and applies automated visual blur on public catalog streams.

R1.3: Configure Concealed Challenge Question
Input: Secret challenge question and secret answer (or hardware serial/IMEI).
Output: Encrypted challenge confirmation.
Processing: Computes SHA-256 hash of plaintext answer; stores hash in database; purges plaintext from memory.

R2: Query and Filter Campus Property Inventory

Description: Enables students and faculty to search, filter, and inspect verified items across all university campuses in real time.

R2.1: Multi-Facet Attribute Filtering
Input: Selected campus zone, category, listing type (Lost/Found), lifecycle status.
Output: Filtered gallery of matching item cards.
Processing: Executes parameterized database query; returns matching active listings sorted by descending timestamp.

R2.2: Free-Text Search
Input: Search query string (e.g., "Campus 6 Library", "Casio Calculator").
Output: Substring-matched items.
Processing: Queries text-indexed title, description, and landmark fields.

R3: Zero-Trust Ownership Verification (ZK-MOP Protocol)

Description: Coordinates the multi-vector ownership claim process, eliminating false claims and social-engineering theft without revealing secrets publicly.

R3.1: Select Verification Proof Vector
Input: Claimant selection of proof type (Roll Match, Serial Hash, Concealed Challenge, Pre-loss Invoice, or Safe Zone Physical Unlock).
Output: Contextual secure proof input form presented to claimant.
Processing: Validates vector appropriateness against item category.

R3.2: Cryptographic Proof Evaluation & Anti-Brute-Force Guard
Input: Claimant's answer string or hardware serial.
Output: Claim verified status or rejection notice.
Processing: Computes SHA-256 hash of submitted value; compares against stored hash. If failed attempts $\ge 3$, claimant account is locked for 24 hours.

R3.3: Finder Review & Claim Approval
Input: Finder approval action.
Output: Status transitioned to CLAIM_APPROVED; claimant notified to retrieve QR pass.
Processing: Commits claim state and authorizes Handover QR Pass generation.

R4: Issue and Validate Cryptographic Safe Zone Handover QR Passes

Description: Issues dynamic, time-expiring QR passes and validates physical handovers via camera optical scanning at Campus Safe Zones.

R4.1: Dynamic QR Handover Pass Generation
Input: Approved item ID, claimant roll number, server secret key.
Output: Dynamic QR code displayed on claimant's smartphone.
Processing: Encodes an HMAC-SHA256 signature containing itemId, claimantRoll, and a 24-hour expiration timestamp.

R4.2: In-Browser Optical QR Handover Scan
Input: Video camera stream from checkpoint officer's device scanning claimant QR.
Output: Handover success prompt with celebration feedback.
Processing: Decodes token payload; validates HMAC signature and expiry; updates item status to RETURNED; logs immutable custody entry in audit ledger.

R5: Anti-Spam Listing Governance and Security Flagging

Description: Prevents malicious listing flooding and community-moderates suspicious postings.

R5.1: Enforce Active Listing Quota & Velocity Cooldown
Input: New listing submission from student account.
Output: Submission accepted or quota rejection notice.
Processing: Checks count of active listings ($\le 3$) and post cooldown time ($\ge 5$ minutes). Rejects submission if violated.

R5.2: Community Security Flagging & Auto-Quarantine
Input: "Flag Listing" action from authenticated student.
Output: Flag confirmation prompt.
Processing: Increments flagCount; if $\ge 3$, automatically quarantines listing and removes it from public view for security audit.

2.3 Non-Functional Requirements

The following characteristics of KIIT-Find cannot be expressed directly as functions, but are essential quality attributes:

  • Performance: The system shall register a scanned item and update the public catalog within 500 milliseconds. The camera QR scanner module shall decode, verify HMAC integrity, and register custody transfer within 1.5 seconds.
  • Reliability: The system shall not lose any transaction state if network connectivity drops midway; all claim transitions are atomically committed in the database before generating handover passes.
  • Availability: The system shall maintain 99.5% uptime 24/7/365 to serve academic hours and residential hostel shifts across all 24 campuses.
  • Usability: The mobile interface shall feature a thumb-accessible floating bottom dock (Home, Catalog, Report, Scanner) allowing one-handed smartphone operation.
  • Security: All users must authenticate via institutional @kiit.ac.in Google OAuth. Secrets are never stored in plaintext; they are hashed with SHA-256. Handover passes expire in 24 hours to prevent replay attacks.
  • Maintainability: Frontend presentation components are strictly decoupled from Next.js REST API routes, allowing visual design updates without impacting business logic.
  • Portability: The system runs on standard web browsers (Google Chrome, Apple Safari, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge) on mobile and desktop without requiring external plugins.

2.4 Goal of Implementation (Future Enhancements)

The following requirements are not needed immediately but are architected into the system foundation for future releases:

  1. AI Semantic Vector Search: Natural language matching between lost item descriptions and found item posts using MongoDB Atlas Vector Search.
  2. Automated SMS / WhatsApp Gateway: Instant notifications dispatched to student mobile numbers via Twilio when an ID card matching their roll number is logged.
  3. Smart Campus Locker Integration: IoT-enabled automated deposit lockers at KSAC and Central Library for physical deposits without security officer presence.
  4. Campus Civic Karma System: A reputation ledger granting verified students "Campus Civic Karma" points for returning lost items, redeemable for university food court or library privileges.
  5. RFID / NFC Gate Beacon Scanner: Automated RFID sensors at campus turnstiles to cross-check reported lost smart cards against physical turnstile taps.
